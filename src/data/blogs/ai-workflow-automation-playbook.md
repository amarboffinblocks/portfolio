## Key Takeaways

- Start automation with high-volume, low-variance tasks.
- Track impact using time saved and error reduction.
- Keep humans in the loop for exceptions.

## Why teams should automate in phases

Most teams fail when they try to automate everything at once. A phased rollout helps teams stabilize each workflow before layering more complexity.

Start with one function where repetitive work is highest, then expand once reliability and ownership are clear.

## Mapping processes before writing automations

Teams often jump directly into tools and lose clarity. First, document triggers, owners, handoffs, and outcomes for each process.

When the map is explicit, choosing the right automation logic becomes much easier and implementation risk drops.

## Measuring outcomes that matter

A practical automation scorecard includes cycle-time reduction, manual effort removed, and quality consistency.

With weekly tracking, teams can quickly identify where workflows need refinement and where new automation opportunities exist.
